Did you know that Alice's Adventures in Wonderland began as an improvised story?
On July 4, 1862, Charles Dodgson, a math lecturer at Oxford who wrote under the pen name Lewis Carroll, took a rowing trip up the Thames with three young sisters, including ten-year-old Alice Liddell, the daughter of the dean of his college.
To entertain them, he made up a tale about a girl who falls down a rabbit hole. Alice begged him to write it down, and he eventually did, giving her a handwritten manuscript titled Alice's Adventures Under Ground in 1864.
When the book was first printed in 1865, illustrator John Tenniel was so unhappy with the print quality that the entire run was recalled.
Only about 22 copies of that original edition are known to survive today, making them some of the most valuable children's books in the world.
